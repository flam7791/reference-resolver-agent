# Generative AI and the public-sector workforce: a short review

*Aurora Institute Working Paper (fictional organisation, sample document for this repository)*

## Summary

Early evidence suggests that generative AI raises productivity in writing and customer-support
tasks, with the largest gains for less experienced workers (Noy and Zhang, 2023; Brynjolfsson,
Li and Raymond, 2025). Longer-run effects on employment depend on how tasks are reorganised
(Autor, 2015; Acemoglu and Restrepo, 2020) and remain contested since early exposure estimates
(Frey and Osborne, 2017). Recent OECD work reviews the labour-market evidence (OECD, 2023) and
the governance questions it raises (OECD, 2019).

Responsible deployment requires documentation of models and data (Mitchell et al., 2019;
Gebru et al., 2021), attention to the limits of large language models (Bender et al., 2021),
and principles that many organisations now share (Jobin, Ienca and Vayena, 2019; Floridi et
al., 2018). Data practices should follow the FAIR principles (Wilkinson et al., 2016).
